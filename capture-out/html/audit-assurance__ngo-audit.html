NGO Audit: A Comprehensive Overview

Introduction to NGO Audit

An NGO audit is an examination of an organization’s financial records, transactions, internal controls, and applicable regulatory compliance. It helps ensure proper financial reporting, responsible use of funds, and compliance with applicable laws and donor requirements.

Objectives of an NGO Audit

  1. Financial Transparency: Verify the accuracy of financial statements and accounting records.

  2. Accountability: Ensure funds are used for their intended purposes.

  3. Regulatory Compliance: Check compliance with applicable tax, corporate, state, and FCRA requirements.

  4. Risk Management: Identify financial irregularities, control weaknesses, and compliance risks.

  5. Internal Controls: Assess systems and procedures for managing funds and maintaining records.

Types of NGO Audits

1. Internal Audit

Reviews financial processes, internal controls, and risk-management systems to identify areas for improvement.

2. Statutory/Financial Audit

An audit required under the applicable law or the NGO's legal structure and circumstances, such as requirements applicable to Section 8 companies or charitable institutions.

3. FCRA Audit

NGOs receiving foreign contributions must maintain prescribed accounts and records and comply with FCRA reporting requirements. The annual return in Form FC-4 includes details of foreign contribution and is accompanied by the required financial statements and certification.

4. Donor-Specific Audit

Donors or grant-making organizations may require an independent audit or financial review to verify the utilization of grant funds according to agreed terms.

Key Areas Covered in an NGO Audit

  • Financial Statements: Review of income, expenditure, assets, liabilities, and accounting records.

  • Internal Controls: Evaluation of financial policies, authorization procedures, and record-keeping.

  • Regulatory Compliance: Review of applicable tax, FCRA, corporate, and other legal requirements.

  • Fund Utilization: Verification that funds are used for permitted and intended purposes.

  • Risk & Irregularities: Identification of discrepancies, control weaknesses, or potential misuse of funds.

NGO Audit Requirements in India

Audit requirements depend on the NGO's legal structure, tax registrations, activities, and applicable laws.

Trusts and Other Charitable Institutions

Charitable or religious trusts and institutions claiming exemption under Sections 11 and 12 may be required to obtain an audit report where the applicable conditions are satisfied. For current filings, Form 10B applies in specified cases, including where total income exceeds ₹5 crore, foreign contribution is received, or income is applied outside India. Form 10BB applies in other specified cases.

Section 8 Companies

Section 8 companies are subject to the applicable audit, accounting, and filing requirements under the Companies Act, 2013.

FCRA-Registered NGOs

NGOs receiving foreign contribution must comply with FCRA accounting and reporting requirements, including annual filing in Form FC-4 and maintenance of prescribed records.

Donor or Grant Requirements

Government agencies, foundations, and other donors may require additional audits or utilization reports based on the terms of a grant or funding agreement.

Why NGO Audit is Important

  • Improves Financial Transparency

  • Strengthens Donor Confidence

  • Supports Regulatory Compliance

  • Identifies Financial Risks

  • Improves Internal Controls

  • Promotes Proper Fund Utilization
